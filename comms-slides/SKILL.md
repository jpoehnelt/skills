# Slides

The audience reads slide text in seconds, so each line states one literal claim. Speaker notes may keep spoken rhythm, but every line says something. Word-level patterns belong to `comms-unslop`.

Repo facts (from AGENTS.md): where decks and notes live, how to preview them, and where the evidence behind the talk lives.

## Shape

- One idea per slide. The title states the slide's claim as a sentence; the body is the evidence.
- Keep slide text light; explanation and caveats go in the notes.
- Read in order, the titles tell the argument.

## One-liners to flag

- Punchline after a fact ("Expect it.").
- Staccato set of three.
- Reversal: "X did not. Y did."
- Aphorism in place of the finding.
- Figure of speech standing in for the claim.
- Setup line that announces without saying anything ("The catch is cost.").
- Slogan title.

## Fix

- Slides first, then notes.
- Rewrite each as its literal claim, or cut it. Cutting is often best.
- Never add a claim the evidence can't back. Cut or soften instead, and say which.
- In notes, keep rhythm that helps delivery; drop setup lines and aphorisms.

## Order and time

- Introduce a thing before anything refers to it, and put the problem right before the method it motivates.
- After a reorder, fix every positional reference in the notes ("we saw", "earlier", "next", slide numbers, outlines).
- To trim, merge slides that make one point rather than cutting content.

## Report

- Before editing: a table of slide, original line, and plain version or "Cut it"; notes lines grouped by pattern; rewrites that need evidence. Ask before applying.
- After: moved slides with old positions, references fixed, lines cut or rewritten, claims softened and why, and the slide count.
